# English Classroom Delivery

Read this reference only when the user requests an English classroom talk, a bilingual presentation package, a timed English speech, or simpler spoken English.

## Delivery brief

Set these values before drafting:

- target audience and course;
- required duration and slide count;
- speaker's comfort level;
- English-only or bilingual deliverables;
- whether the user will read, memorize, or use cue cards;
- requested opening, names, thanks, and closing language.

## Time and word budget

Use an explicit estimate instead of guessing after the script is written.

| Speaker profile | Planning rate |
|---|---:|
| cautious learner, unfamiliar topic | 100—110 words per minute |
| general classroom delivery | 110—120 words per minute |
| fluent, well-rehearsed delivery | 120—130 words per minute |

Reserve time for questions, pauses, slide changes, and breathing. As a practical starting point, an 8—10 minute classroom report usually targets about **850—1,050 English words** at 110—120 words per minute with several pauses. Recalculate for the actual number of questions and the user's speaking pace.

For each slide, provide a suggested duration. The per-slide times must add up to the total target. Keep a small safety margin rather than writing to the absolute maximum.

## 简单英语 standard

Unless the user or subject requires more advanced language, target A2—B1 spoken English:

- prefer common verbs: `use`, `show`, `help`, `start`, `stop`, `change`, `make`, `keep`;
- prefer short sentences, usually one main idea per sentence;
- reduce noun-heavy phrases and nested clauses;
- use contractions only when they are easy for the speaker to pronounce;
- keep essential academic terms, then explain each once in simple words;
- choose words that are easy to say and remember, not words that merely sound academic.

Example:

- harder: `This mechanism reduces behavioral inertia during task reactivation.`
- spoken: `This makes it easier to start the task again.`

Do not simplify away the argument. Simple wording must preserve cause, uncertainty, contrast, and evidence strength.

## Opening pattern

When the user needs a group or course opening, adapt this structure:

```text
Good morning, everyone.
Today I will give our group's presentation, and I am happy to learn with you.
First, I want to thank our teacher for this chance. It lets me practice and share what I have learned.
I promise, this is not a joke.
```

Then state the surprising idea, ask the central question, introduce the theme anchor, and promise what the audience will know or be able to do by the end.

## Slide words and speech

The slide is a visual map; the script is the spoken explanation.

For each slide:

- use one conclusion-led title;
- show only the exact keywords, short claims, steps, numbers, contrasts, or diagram labels needed to see the structure;
- place the slide's question on screen only when it helps the audience follow the reasoning;
- write a speech section that expands the slide with a scene, explanation, marker, question, or transition;
- ensure every visible question is actually spoken.

Avoid full English paragraphs on slides. Do not repeat every on-screen phrase word for word in the script.

## Bilingual delivery package

When the user requests translation or says the English is hard to understand, deliver and synchronize:

1. the English PPT or paste-ready slide copy;
2. the page-aligned English speech script;
3. a matching `中文译文`;
4. a per-slide timing table;
5. concise `使用说明` for presenting, rehearsing, and switching pages;
6. `PPT 演讲者备注` when the deck format supports notes.

Translate for understanding rather than word-for-word sameness. Preserve claim strength, questions, story order, theme wording, and closing intent.

## Revision loop

After any request such as “shorter,” “about 60 words less,” or “use easier vocabulary”:

1. record the previous and target word counts;
2. protect the main claim, story, reasoning chain, practical method, questions, theme loop, and closing;
3. apply the compression order from `voice-and-storytelling.md`;
4. recount words and recalculate timing;
5. update the external script, Chinese translation, timing guide, and PPT speaker notes together;
6. re-render every changed file and inspect it again.

## Compact document layout

Keep the script easy to print and rehearse. Use clear slide labels, readable type, tight but comfortable paragraph spacing, and page breaks that keep each slide section intact. Two slide sections may share one page when they remain readable. Never achieve compactness by shrinking text below a practical reading size.

## Final checks

- The word count fits the planned duration.
- Vocabulary matches the speaker's level.
- Difficult terms are explained once in simple language.
- Slide text is sparse and specific.
- PPT notes, English script, Chinese translation, and timing are synchronized.
- The opening includes required identity and thanks before the guarantee.
- Questions, pauses, language markers, and theme returns are audible.
- The close completes gratitude, respect, gained capability, expectation, and theme return.
- Every PPT slide and document page has been rendered and visually inspected.
